El imperativo

The imperative: how to tell someone what to do. In this unit we focus on tú.

Part 1 · What is it for?

What is the imperative?

We use the imperative to tell someone what to do.

¡Bebe agua!Drink water!
Abre el libro.Open the book.
Part 1 · What is it for?

We use it to…

Give advice

Bebe mucha agua.Drink a lot of water.
Duerme ocho horas.Sleep eight hours.

Give instructions

Abre el libro en la página 117.Open your book at page 117.
Mezcla dos gotas con tu crema.Mix two drops with your cream.
Part 1 · What is it for?

We also use it to…

Ask for something

Pásame la sal, por favor.Pass me the salt, please.
Cierra la ventana, por favor.Close the window, please.

Invite and offer

Pasa y siéntate.Come in and sit down.
Toma un poco más.Have some more.

Give orders

¡Escucha!Listen!
¡Ven aquí!Come here!
Part 1 · English and Spanish

One form or four?

English: one form

The same form for everybody.

Eat!

Spanish: it depends on who you talk to

tú: ¡come!usted: ¡coma!vosotros: ¡comed!ustedes: ¡coman!

In this unit we mainly use tú: a friend, a classmate, your family.

Part 1 · English and Spanish

Three more things

1. No subject

Like in English, we don't say the subject.

Close the door.
Cierra la puerta.
Tú cierra la puerta.

2. It is polite

With por favor, the imperative is normal and polite with friends and family.

Could you pass me the salt?
Pásame la sal, por favor.

3. Two exclamation marks

Spanish opens and closes the exclamation.

Come here!
¡Ven aquí!

Part 2

How do we form it?

Four steps:

  1. Regular verbs
  2. Stem-changing verbs
  3. Eight irregular verbs
  4. Adding pronouns
Step 1 · Regular verbs

The rule

tú imperative = present tense of él / ella

InfinitivePresent (él / ella)Imperative (tú)English
comprarél compra¡Compra!Buy!
comerél come¡Come!Eat!
vivirél vive¡Vive!Live!
Step 1 · Regular verbs

Another way to see it (as in the book)

Take the tú form of the present and remove the -s.

compras → compra
comes → come
vives → vive

Both tricks give the same result. Choose the one you prefer.

Step 2 · Stem-changing verbs

The change stays

If the verb changes in the present, it also changes in the imperative. The rule from step 1 still works: él duerme → ¡duerme!

e → iepensar → piensacerrar → cierraempezar → empieza
o → uedormir → duermevolver → vuelvecontar → cuenta
u → uejugar → juega
e → ipedir → piderepetir → repite
Step 3 · Irregular verbs

The -go trick

Six irregular verbs have a yo form that ends in -go. Remove -go and you have the imperative.

teneryo tengotenhave
poneryo pongoponput
saliryo salgosalgo out, leave
veniryo vengovencome
deciryo digodisay, tell
haceryo hago + zhazdo, make
Step 3 · Irregular verbs

Two more, with no trick

irvego
sersébe

Learn all eight as one long word:

VEN·DI·SAL·HAZ·TEN·VE·PON·SÉ
Step 3 · Irregular verbs

Be careful

  • ve is the imperative of ir and also of ver. Go! / Look!
  • sé (ser) has a written accent. Be good → Sé bueno.
  • sal (salir) is also the Spanish word for salt.
  • The -go trick only works for these six verbs:
    traer (traigo) → trae  ·  oír (oigo) → oye
Step 4 · Pronouns

After the verb, joined to it

In English the pronoun comes after the verb as a separate word. In Spanish it also comes after the verb, but it is joined to it: one word.

EnglishEspañolPronoun
Eat it. (el bocadillo)Cómelo.lo (direct object)
Moisturise them. (los pies)Hidrátalos.los (direct object)
Tell me.Dime.me (indirect object)
Step 4 · Pronouns

Reflexive verbs: te + the

English uses your. Spanish uses te and the article (el, la, los, las).

Wash your hands.Lávate las manos.
Brush your teeth.Lávate los dientes.
Sit down.Siéntate.
Get up.Levántate.
Put on sunscreen.Ponte protector solar.
Relax.Relájate.
Step 4 · Pronouns

Two pronouns: person first, then thing

The order is the opposite of English.

Give it to me.

Da + me + lo

= Dámelo

Tell it to me.

Di + me + lo

= Dímelo

Extra: le + lo becomes se lo. Give it to him → Dáselo.

Step 4 · Pronouns

The written accent (la tilde)

The stressed syllable of the verb does not move. When we add syllables at the end, we often need a written accent.

Verb + pronounsAccent?Examples
