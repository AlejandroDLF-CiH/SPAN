Part 1 · How does it work?

What are possessives?

They show who something belongs to, or who someone is related to.

mi hermanamy sister (family)
mis amigosmy friends (people around you)
mi libromy book (things you have)
mi callemy street (other relations)

Part 1 · How does it work?

The trick: just add -s

Plural thing → add -s. Masculine or feminine → no change.

Masculine or feminine: same word

mi padre · mi madre

One or many: add -s

mi hermano · mis hermanos

English my never changes: my brother · my brothers. Spanish does.

Part 2 · The key rule

Count the things, not the owners

This is the most important idea of the lesson.

Part 2 · The key rule

English and Spanish look at different things

English looks at the owner

Is the owner a man or a woman?

his father · her father

Spanish looks at the thing

Is it one thing or more than one?

su padre · sus padres

Part 2 · The key rule

Su has many meanings

The context tells you who the owner is.

su = hisheritsyour (usted)their
Pablo y su hermanaPablo and his sister
Mis abuelos y su casaMy grandparents and their house
Part 3 · Two small rules

tu or tú?

tú (with an accent) = you

Tú eres mi amigo.

You are my friend.

tu (no accent) = your

¿Cómo se llama tu hermano?

What is your brother's name?

In English they are also two different words: you and your.

Part 3 · Two small rules

No article before mi, tu, su

Just like English: you can't say the my cousin.
