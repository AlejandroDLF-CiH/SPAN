What is verb conjugation?

Verb conjugation means changing the form of a verb according to the subject.

English

The verb changes, but often only a little.

am is are

Spanish

The verb normally changes more clearly according to the subject.

We usually organize Spanish verb conjugation into six basic slots. Some slots may share the same form.

BE and SER

Ser is one of the Spanish verbs that mean “to be.”

A special case: usted and ustedes

What do you notice?

Pronoun SER
usted es
él / ella es
ustedes son
ellos / ellas son
usted and ustedes are second-person pronouns.
But they use the same verb forms as the third person:
usted → es, like él / ella → es
ustedes → son, like ellos / ellas → son

Now let’s use ser in sentences. Do we always need to say the subject?

Spanish often leaves out the subject

Compare the sentences. In Spanish, both versions are correct when the meaning is clear.

EnglishSpanish: with the subjectSpanish: without the subject
I am a teacher.Yo soy profesor.Soy profesor.
You are a student.Tú eres estudiante.Eres estudiante.
She is Spanish.Ella es española.Es española.
We are students.Nosotros somos estudiantes.Somos estudiantes.
You are students.Vosotros sois estudiantes.Sois estudiantes.
They are from Hong Kong.Ellos son de Hong Kong.Son de Hong Kong.
In English, we normally need a subject:
I am a teacher.
In Spanish, we can say Soy profesor.
We do not need to say yo.
We often leave out the subject when the verb form or the context makes the meaning clear.

— ¿De dónde es María?
Where is María from?
— Es de España.
She is from Spain.

We already know we are talking about María, so we do not need to repeat María or ella.

When do we include the subject?

1. To make it clear who we mean

María es profesora. Pedro es estudiante.
María is a teacher. Pedro is a student.

We name the subjects to make it clear who we are talking about. If both people are in the conversation, Es estudiante may not tell us who we mean.

2. To emphasize or contrast

Yo soy profesor. Tú eres estudiante.
I am a teacher. You are a student.

We include yo and tú to emphasize the difference between us.

In Spanish, subjects can be left out in all persons—singular and plural.

Leave out the subject when the meaning is clear. Include it when needed for clarity, emphasis or contrast.
