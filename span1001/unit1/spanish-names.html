Where do given names come from?

Spanish given names can come from many sources. Traditionally, family and religion have played an important role.

Nicknames are very common

In everyday Spanish, many traditional given names have familiar short forms or nicknames.

Ignacio → Nacho
Dolores → Lola / Loli / Lolita
José → Pepe
Francisco → Paco
Juan José → Juanjo
Miguel Ángel
This is a compound given name, not “first name + middle name”.
A nickname may look very different from the original name, so do not always expect a simple shortened form.

What about baptismal names?

Historically, some people received several religious or baptismal given names.

That can make the complete formal name much longer than the name used in everyday life.

Everyday identity
Pablo Ruiz Picasso

A person can have several given names in formal or religious records but use only one in daily life.

For beginners, the key idea is simple: a long formal name does not mean that every part is used every day.

How do the two surnames work?

Traditionally, a child receives one surname from the father and one surname from the mother.

Father

+
Mother
Laura López Martín

In modern Spain, parents can agree on the order of the surnames. The traditional father-first pattern is still a useful starting point for understanding the system.

Names can be much longer

A person may have:

  • more than one given name,
  • two ordinary surnames,
  • and, historically, additional religious names in formal records.
Given name(s)
+
First surname + Second surname

Daily usage is usually much shorter than the complete legal or historical form.
