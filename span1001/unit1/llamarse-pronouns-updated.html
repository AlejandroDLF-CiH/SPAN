Spanish infinitives and -se

English Spanish

Spanish verbs in the infinitive normally end in one of three ways:

-ar
-er
-ir

Verbs we already know

ser

tener

Other examples

hablar (speak)

Today we are going to learn our third Spanish verb:
llamarse

This verb looks a little different.

llamar + se → llamarse
What is the -se?

From yo, tú, él... to me, te, se...

The -se tells us that this verb is used with a small pronoun.

llamarse literally means to call oneself.
Me llamo Juan.
Literally: I call myself Juan.
Natural English: My name is Juan.

Just like English has I / me or he / him, Spanish also uses different pronoun forms in different situations.
The forms are not exactly the same as English, but the basic idea is similar.

The conjugation of LLAMARSE

With llamarse, two things change: the pronoun and the verb.

Subject Pronoun LLAMARSE
yo me me llamo
tú te te llamas
él / ella / usted se se llama
nosotros / nosotras nos nos llamamos
vosotros / vosotras os os llamáis
ellos / ellas / ustedes se se llaman
Two steps:
1. Choose the pronoun: me, te, se, nos, os, se
2. Change the verb: llamo, llamas, llama, llamamos, llamáis, llaman
yo → me + llamo → Me llamo Juan.

Using LLAMARSE

Asking someone's name

¿Cómo te llamas? What is your name?
Me llamo Alejandro. My name is Alejandro.
¿Cómo se llama? What is his / her / your name?
Se llama María. Her name is María.
With llamarse, the small pronoun changes with the subject:
me, te, se, nos, os, se.
You will see more Spanish verbs with -se later. The same basic idea will help you understand them.
