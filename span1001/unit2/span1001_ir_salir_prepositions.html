Quick review

IR and SALIR:
Which preposition?

We have seen expressions such as ir al cine, ir de compras, salir a cenar and salir con amigos. Why do we use a, de or con?

Good news: for the expressions we are learning now, there are a few simple patterns that can help you.
Pattern 1

IR A + place / event

IR A tells us where you go or what event you go to.
ir a la playa
to go to the beach
ir al museo
to go to the museum
ir a un concierto
to go to a concert
ir a una exposición
to go to an exhibition
ir al cine
to go to the cinema
ir al teatro
to go to the theatre

Remember: a + el = al. For example: a + el museo → al museo.

Pattern 2

IR DE + activity

IR DE tells us what kind of activity you go out to do.
ir de compras
to go shopping
ir de excursión
to go on an excursion / trip
Think: What activity are you doing?

Do not think simply “a = concrete” and “de = abstract”. It is more useful to remember these expressions as patterns.

Pattern 3

SALIR A + infinitive

SALIR A + verb = to go out to do something
salir a cenar
to go out for dinner / to go out to have dinner
salir a bailar
to go out dancing
Easy clue: after a, we have an infinitive: cenar, bailar…
Question: What do you go out to do?
A useful expression

SALIR DE NOCHE

salir de noche = to go out at night

For now, learn salir de noche as one useful expression.

You will also see salir de + place with the meaning “to leave / come out of a place”: salir de casa (leave home), salir del trabajo (leave work). So we do not need one general “de = abstract” rule.

Pattern 4

SALIR CON + person / people

SALIR CON tells us who you go out with.
salir con amigos/as
to go out with friends
salir con mis compañeros/as
to go out with my classmates
Question: Who do you go out with?
