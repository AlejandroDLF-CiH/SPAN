01 · Start with English

The red one / a red one

Avoid repeating a noun that both people understand.

Both people must know what kind of thing we are talking about.
Here, we are talking about T-shirts: camisetas.

English

red T-shirt
black T-shirt

I want the red T-shirt.

I want a red T-shirt.

Spanish

We can do something similar in Spanish.

Quiero la camiseta roja.
Quiero una camiseta roja.

Click to compare the shorter forms.

In Spanish, we do not add another word after the adjective for English one / ones.

02 · Meaning

Which particular one?

The noun is understood in both cases. The article changes the meaning.

Both people are talking about T-shirts.

Definite · the

el · la · los · las

the red T-shirt
the black T-shirt

You see a red T-shirt and a black T-shirt. You choose the red one.

Quiero la roja.

I want the red one.

The other person can identify which particular item you mean.

Indefinite · a / some

uno · una · unos · unas

a red T-shirt
another red T-shirt
another red T-shirt

The shop has many T-shirts. You want a red one, but you have not chosen a particular one.

Quiero una roja.

I want a red one.

You do not expect the other person to identify a particular item yet.

More than one

Quiero las rojas.

I want the red ones.

The other person can identify which T-shirts.

Quiero unas rojas.

I want some red ones.

You are asking for some red T-shirts, without identifying a particular group.

03 · Gender and number

The noun disappears. Agreement stays.

The remaining forms must agree with the noun we leave out.

The noun is not there, but its gender and number still matter.
Blue words stay. Watch un → uno.

One form to remember: un → uno

un jersey rojo → uno rojo

Before a masculine singular noun, use un. When that noun is left out in this pattern, use uno.

Here, uno stands in for the masculine singular noun.

04 · A final reminder

Some adjectives share one gender form

Not all adjectives change like rojo / roja.

Azul, verde and grande have the same form for masculine and feminine nouns. Their form changes only for number.

The article still shows gender and number.

The same rule with indefinite forms

un jersey azul → uno azul

una camiseta azul → una azul

unos zapatos azules → unos azules

unas sandalias azules → unas azules

We are looking at T-shirts.

La camiseta azul es muy pequeña.

The blue T-shirt is very small.

La and pequeña are feminine singular. Azul has the same form for both genders.

05 · Practice

Say it, then check

In every task, both people know what kind of items they are talking about.

Leave out the noun

Keep the original meaning. Change un to uno when needed.

A1

Quiero la camiseta roja.

Rewrite the whole sentence without the noun.

Quiero la roja.

A2

Quiero un jersey negro.

Rewrite the whole sentence without the noun.

Quiero uno negro.

A3

Los zapatos rojos son bonitos.

Rewrite the whole sentence without the noun.

Los rojos son bonitos.

A4

Quiero unas maletas pequeñas.

Rewrite the whole sentence without the noun.

Quiero unas pequeñas.

Decide whether the listener can identify a particular item.

06 · Speak

Choose an item. Leave out the noun.

Both people can see the same items. Choose one and say what you want.

You are both looking at T-shirts.

Your sentence
Quiero ….

Choose an item. Say your sentence before showing the model.

Extra: “these red ones”

These is a demonstrative, not an article. It can be used in a similar way.

Talking about T-shirts:

Quiero estas rojas.

I want these red ones.

Talking about jumpers:

Quiero estos rojos.

I want these red ones.

Remember: the noun must be understood. Choose the meaning, then keep the right gender and number.
