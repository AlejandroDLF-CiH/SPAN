HAY means there is or there are.
One form in Spanish

HAY = there is / there are

Hay un museo.
There is a museum.
Hay tres museos.
There are three museums.
English changes: there is / there are.
Spanish uses the same form: HAY.
Hay un restaurante.
Hay dos restaurantes.
Hay muchos restaurantes.
Where does HAY come from?

HAY ← HABER

HABER → HAY

Hay is a form of the verb haber.

For now, you only need to remember:
HAY = there is / there are.

We do not need the other forms of haber yet.

What comes after HAY?

HAY introduces something new

Hay normally introduces a person or thing that is not specific or not identified yet.

HAY + un / una + singular noun
Hay un museo.
Hay una universidad.
HAY + plural noun
Hay museos.
Hay restaurantes.
HAY + number + noun
Hay tres museos.
Hay dos universidades.
HAY + quantity + noun
Hay muchos restaurantes.
Hay muchas cafeterías.
Important

HAY and definite articles

After HAY, we normally do NOT use el, la, los, las.
✗ Hay el museo en el centro.

Here, el museo refers to a specific, identified museum.

✓ Hay un museo en el centro.

un museo introduces a museum that has not been identified yet.

HAY normally introduces something new or unidentified.
HAY vs. ESTAR

Existence or location?

HAYESTAR
Main ideaWhat is there?Where is it?
ExampleHay un museo en el centro.El museo está en el centro.
EnglishThere is a museum in the city centre.The museum is in the city centre.
HAY introduces it.    ESTAR locates it.

A useful first rule: hay often introduces something new; estar often gives the location of something already identified.

Summary

What do we remember?

HAY = there is / there are
HAY is the same with singular and plural nouns.
HAY normally introduces something new or unidentified: un/una + singular noun, a plural noun, or a noun with a number/quantity.
HAY normally does not introduce a specific noun with el / la / los / las.
HAY introduces it.   ESTAR locates it.
